## Screen Problems

So after holding off on repairing my still "functional" phone[^4] I finally succumb to the repair
gods after ominous black splodges started expanding from the top left corner of my screen.
Experiencing the night light struggling to turn off for a minute after switching on the phone only
enhanced my motivation to *at least* fix this screen issue, as personal experience dictates that a
working display is a fairly highly vital for daily use of a mobile phone. The accelerometer, torch,
and speaker being broken I can live with.

## Repair

After purchase of a replacement screen[^5] (of questionable quality) and recruiting a friend for
moral support alongside borrowing her tools I was ready to start.

### Removing Screen

There are three important parts to removing a phone screen:

- Heat: To soften the adhesive bonding the screen to the phone
- Suction cup: To slightly lift the screen off
- Thin piece of plastic: To pry the screen off via the slight gap

This process was made easier by virtue of having already taken off the screen once before and not
needing the original screen to work, which turned out to be quite handy as I accidentally levered
some of the screen apart rather than off of the phone!

After several rounds of heating and prying the display finally came off and I got access to the guts
of my phone. Having a poke around the broken screen me and my friend found corrosion in the top left
corner, which is the likely cause of the *ominous* black spots.

*I would not recommend using a phone like this but there is nothing stopping you.*

### Removing Adhesive

After testing that the new screen did in fact work, the (***tedious***) task of removing old
adhesive began, so I could put in new adhesive for the new screen. A process that mildly tested my
sanity, not helped by the technician (who fixed *the last* broken screen) seemingly not getting
around to the removal of old adhesive phase.

{{< image path="with_adhesive.jpg" dimensions="300x" \
alt="Black textured adhesive around the inner perimeter of a phones inside" >}}

This was by far the longest part of the repair as the adhesive was very degraded, needing to be
scraped off instead of peeled. Some lovely comments made by me during the process are
"dkhgfsuifhusegfisdhf" and "It gets no better after an hour".

### Applying Adhesive

This is simple, *in theory*. Applying adhesive glue or strips around the perimeter of a phone to
attach the screen.

Application of adhesive was complicated by my friend supplying outdated glue. The main symptom of
"outdatedness" was the viscosity of the glue being comparable to a block of wood. Diligent observers
may note that a human cannot shape a block of wood with their fingers. What followed was the best
worst idea of the process as the words "Where's the vice" left my mouth and I began searching for a
G-clamp[^1].

Initially this worked okay, I was able to somewhat extract glue from the intended orifice. It was
when I loosened the G-clamp that my folly came to light. There was now a second hold in the glue
tube. Unexpectedly, this lead to the most efficient method of glue extraction. Squeezing out
droplets and placing them with tweezers on the phone.

{{< image path="broken_glue.jpg" dimensions="200x" \
alt="Tube of half empty yellow glue with a rift in the bottom half with black tweezers beneath" >}}

### Securing Screen

To ensure the screen and the phone remain in matrimony, pressure is required for the first few
hours. A bag full of elastic bands was perfectly able to provide such support in the form of phone
Shibari[^2], during which my phone was amusingly somewhat usable.

{{< image path="screen_secure.jpg" dimensions="300x" \
alt="A phone wrapped in a grid of elastic bands" >}}

## Closing Remarks

The experience was one I would call interesting rather than enjoyable, mostly due to the old
adhesive and lack of functioning glue. Sans those elements this is something I would recommend to
anyone who does not mind tinkering. After all, I did get a working phone in the end.

Regarding the replacement phone screen, I would describe it as adequate. With large bezels (perhaps
due to not having any pixels behind the curved glass?) and an imperfection in the middle left of the
screen. Furthermore, the night light looks outright *bad* compared to my memory of the stock screen.
It works, but if I were to do it again I would look elsewhere for a screen.

I performed this repair in my local [hackspace](https://www.hackspace.org.uk/)[^3], which helped
immensely with tools for heating and finding competent people to help me with the process.
